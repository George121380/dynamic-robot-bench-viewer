Technical report on predicting bowling motion from 21 points

Task definition, implementations, experiments and unresolved causes

For researchers and engineers who have not followed the project. The report introduces the task before discussing models, evidence and implementation details.

The models can fit a small set of training trajectories accurately, but do not reliably predict when pins should move or how they should respond to collisions in new scenes. Restricting access to later robot plans repairs a specific structural inconsistency. Shared object processing reduces some precontact drift but worsens postcontact displacement accuracy on fresh heldout scenes. A faithful reproduction of the complete original paper has not been achieved.

1 Conclusions supported by the completed work

The initial question was whether a ball center and two endpoints per pin are enough to learn motion conditioned on robot actions. Completed work includes data preparation, trajectory compression checks, direct coordinate prediction, small-set fitting, temporal visibility controls, contact diagnostics and a shared-object comparison using fresh evaluation scenes. This report reorganizes completed evidence; it adds no training or physics experiments.[E1][E2][E4][E6][E7][E8]

  • Confirmed: models predict substantial pin motion before the first recorded nonsupport contact. Feeding these erroneous positions into later predictions amplifies errors.
  • Confirmed: later nominal robot plans influence earlier predictions in the original fully connected model. Restricting temporal visibility removes this dependence but does not remove drift.
  • Confirmed: on fresh heldout scenes, the shared-object package improves precontact error by 39.1% while worsening postcontact increment error by 8.9%. The latter worsens for all three fixed training seeds.
  • Unresolved: the relative roles of incomplete input state, limited independent trajectories, the training objective, learning difficulty and the absence of within-chunk sequential state updates. No single remaining cause has been established.
Decision: the tested object-structure package fails the continuation criterion fixed before training. End repeated tuning of this version. Preserve its local benefit and use a discriminating diagnostic before committing to another architecture.

No added static-motion penalty, ground-truth contact input, hard motion switch, rigid projection or output correction was used in the final comparisons. Position errors are computed from saved original predictions.[E6][E8]

2 What the model is asked to predict

A robot pushes a ball toward ten pins. Given past observations, the current scene and a planned robot motion, the model predicts ball and pin coordinates over the next few seconds. It does not learn to select robot actions in these experiments.

Each pin has two fixed semantic axis endpoints,150 mm apart, and the ball has one center point:21 scene points in total. The endpoints differ from mesh extrema by at most approximately 0.261 mm. They describe pin position and axis direction but do not directly encode rotation about that axis; the ball center does not directly encode spin. Whether missing state is decisive remains untested. Twenty additional gripper points describe the robot.[E1]

Coordinates and identities come from simulator state, not visual tracking. The study therefore examines prediction with accurate coordinates. It does not evaluate camera noise, occlusion, lost tracks or real-robot operation.[E1]

Past/current points→Known robot plan→Predict next2s of scene points

The initially supplied paper link is UNITAS; pointwam_bowling is a historical directory name. The initial implementation is a simplified independent version with visual and language components removed. Later fully connected and shared-object predictors are separate controls. Their results do not establish the performance of the complete UNITAS method.[E0][E1]

At query time 0, the five allowed inputs are past scene points, current scene points, past robot points, current robot points and a nominal future robot plan. History consists of ten frames from−1.0 to−0.1 s. The plan is generated from current robot state and fixed commands in an independent contact-disabled simulation. It is a legitimately available plan, not future executed robot ground truth.[E1][E4]

The model predicts twenty frames at 10 Hz, from 0.1 to 2.0 s. A four-second forecast uses two chunks. The second receives the first chunk's predicted scene history and current state plus the original nominal robot plan; it receives no new scene truth. Training labels cover only 2 s. Four-second evaluation therefore also tests operation on self-generated inputs.[E4][E6][E8]

Known state at0s → forecast0–2s→Predicted scene at2s becomes input→Forecast2–4s without new scene truth

The historical query is defined retrospectively as 0.1 s before the first positive-force robot–ball contact, with sufficient history and future required. It is not shifted according to pin impact. The experiment does not solve online query triggering. Fresh evaluation retains every predetermined physical draw, whereas old accepted training collection included visual eligibility filtering; identical accepted-data distributions are not claimed.[E8]

4 What a correct prediction means

Average displacement error, abbreviated ADE, is the mean Euclidean distance between predicted and true points at saved future times. Final displacement error, FDE, measures the final frame. An ADE of 10 mm means an average position discrepancy of 1 cm under the specified averaging rule. It is not an accuracy percentage. Different horizons, groups and averaging rules are not interchangeable.

Training squares and averages predicted-minus-true coordinate differences, then adjusts model parameters to reduce this error. Ball, moving-pin and static-pin groups are computed separately and present groups have equal training weight. A moving pin exceeds10mm endpoint displacement within the evaluated horizon; static means below this threshold, not exactly motionless or uncontacted. Future truth defines supervision and scoring, never predictor inputs. Validation selection uses ball/moving/static ADE weights0.4/0.4/0.2, renormalized over present groups. Groups can differ between two- and four-second horizons.[E4][E8]

A single average can hide both premature movement and incorrect collision response. The final experiment uses two co-primary measures. Precontact endpoint error uses frames strictly before each pin's first nonsupport event and excludes pins contacted before the query. Ball, other pins, robot and wall contacts count; support-surface contact does not.[E7][E8]

Postcontact increment error compares predicted and true endpoint changes over the complete next half-second, relative to the saved frame at or immediately before contact. It reduces confounding by an already accumulated position offset. Exactly five 10 Hz frames strictly after contact must be available; otherwise the metric is unavailable rather than zero.[E7][E9]

Events use active simulation constraints and positive normal force above 10⁻⁶N at 2 ms integration-start timestamps. Geometric proximity is not treated as contact. Scores average frames within pin, pins within episode, then the three fixed training seeds; episodes have equal weight. Predictions are not coordinate-averaged into an ensemble. A stationary reference holds every point at its current position; a constant-velocity reference extrapolates a velocity estimated from past coordinates. These expose apparent gains caused mainly by suppressing motion.[E7][E8][E9]

The old original two-second validation window contains new contact in only 4 episodes and 14 pins, with no complete postcontact half-second for any pin. It cannot evaluate complete responses. This is not absence of training collisions: 879 training windows from 95 episodes contain new contact. Four-second fresh evaluation addresses this coverage problem.[E7][E8]

5.1 Check trajectory compression before future prediction

The original route first compresses a point trajectory into a shorter numerical representation, then reconstructs it. This component is called a tokenizer in code. It already receives true future coordinates during this check, so reconstruction is not forecasting.[E1][E2]

Two 5000-update runs failed the chosen reconstruction goal. The original selected model has future ball/moving-pin ADE 16.114/13.047 mm and reconstructs an actually zero-motion future with 7.587 mm ADE. At the final 5000-step checkpoint, the inspected decoder activations are all zero and different internal representations produce identical reconstruction. This final checkpoint is different from the selected 2000-step model. Lower learning rate avoids the inspected final collapse but leaves substantial reconstruction error.[E1][E2]

Changing the time representation reduces real-small-set group-mean reconstruction error 20.094→11.323 mm, a 43.6% improvement, while static-pin error worsens. Increasing width also gives only partial improvement. The 1 mm ADE/3 mm FDE target is a project engineering choice, not a paper requirement or a necessary condition for every useful model.[E2][E3]

A separate lossless-coordinate control stores all samples in time order without learned trajectory compression, then trains the existing more complex dynamics network. After2000updates on the same eight-episode88window set, ball/moving/static ADE remains29.308/30.581/9.371mm and the curve is still improving. Compression is therefore not the only obstacle; the limited run does not prove insufficient network capacity. The subsequent direct-coordinate predictor tests small-set fitting more thoroughly.[E2]

5.2 Bypass compression and test small-set fitting

The direct-coordinate control uses three fully connected hidden layers of width 512 and approximately 2.48 million parameters. It outputs displacements for 21 points at 20 future times. On all 88 windows from eight training episodes, ball/moving-pin/static-pin ADE reaches 0.440/0.133/0.194 mm. The computational pipeline can fit these samples and near-stationary outputs. Full-data models still drift on unseen scenes. Small-set fitting does not prove sufficient state, collision identifiability or generalization.[E4]

5.3 Premature movement is measurable before physical contact

In the earlier 16 validation episodes, native true coordinates move by at most 0.324 mm before each first nonsupport contact. Every one of 12 inspected checkpoints predicts more than 5 mm movement for all 160 pins in their observable precontact intervals. This uses physical event times and raw world coordinates, not a visual impression or a fixed-first-second measure.[E7]

6 Why later actions must not change an earlier prediction

A temporal prefix is the segment from now up to a specified time. Under the constraint used here, the 0.5 s output can use robot plans only through 0.5 s; the 1.5 s output can use plans through 1.5 s. All outputs still use past and current observations.

Consider two plans identical for the first second, followed by continuing versus stopping. With the same initial state and first-second commands, the first-second physical execution is identical. The predictor must likewise keep its first-second outputs unchanged. The control below illustrates which planned frames are visible at each output time; it is a schematic, not a measured trajectory.

Schematic only; not experimental trajectories
Predicted time

The original predictor reads the complete two-second plan before outputting all frames. In controller-generated pairs with exact first-second command, nominal-plan and physical-state agreement, its first-second pin predictions change by 4.399 mm on average. This concerns dependence on a legal plan input, not future scene-ground-truth leakage.[E5][E6]

The control preserves parameters and time-specific output heads but evaluates each output with only its allowed plan prefix. Later plan features are zeroed after training-only standardization; raw world coordinates are not simply set to zero. Matched initialization, samples, loss and 3×15000 updates per arm are used. Early dependence becomes exactly zero; old-validation first-second motion over 10 mm decreases 49.38%→33.54%. Motion over 2 mm remains 100%, and two-second weighted error worsens 22.310→23.330 mm. Four-second static error does not improve.[E6]

Temporal visibility guarantees a consistency property. It does not tell the model when contact occurs, force pins to remain still, guarantee accuracy, or implement sequential physical state updates.

7 How errors accumulate through predicted inputs

An incorrect first chunk becomes the second chunk's assumed observation. We diagnose this by restarting frozen models from true observations at 2 s and comparing the same 2–4 s interval.

An earlier fixed-static-pin comparison gives 50.844 mm error with original inputs,27.657 mm after correcting scene inputs,45.988 mm after correcting the robot input block, and 23.095 mm after both. Scene feedback is a substantial amplifier. The replacements interact and their gains are not additive causal percentages.[E5]

The final study repeats a jointly observed boundary diagnostic: replace scene history/current and robot history/current, then regenerate the nominal plan from that robot boundary. Both conditions below score the identical true 2–4 s interval.[E8][E9]

Postcontact increment errors remain 41.199/39.702 mm against 43.358 mm true response. Accumulation therefore does not explain all failure. The shifted query has 28 complete-postcontact episodes rather than the original query's 31; it cannot replace the co-primary result or isolate scene versus robot causes.[E8][E9]

8 Design of the fresh shared-object comparison

The new model treats the ball, ten pins and robot as objects. Pins share an encoder and output rules. Two attention blocks learn how much information each object should receive from others, using relative current positions and distances. There are no true contact inputs or hard distance switches.[E8][E11]

Both models retain temporal prefixes and output twenty future slots in parallel. Within a two-second chunk, neither explicitly predicts a next ball state and then uses that predicted state to update pins. Sharing, relative geometry and pooled normalization change together, so this tests a package rather than one mechanism.[E8][E11]

Yet the episode-macro fraction of pins ever predicted to move beyond 5 mm anywhere in their observable precontact interval remains 100%→98.36%. This is neither a fixed-first-second rate nor a fraction of independent episodes. Reduced ADE does not remove premature motion.[E8]

Precontact and postcontact intervals lie on opposite sides of zero. They quantify episode uncertainty conditional on these three seeds, not all training randomness, repeated development choices or real-world environments.

The predeclared continuation rule requires improvement in both metrics for all seeds, at least 10%mean reductions, both interval upper bounds below zero, and ball ADE worsening no more than 10%. Coverage requires 48 valid and 16 complete-postcontact episodes. Coverage passes and ball worsening is 3.69%, but all three postcontact improvement conditions fail. The result is a failure, not inconclusive support. This is a project decision rule, not a universal physical success criterion.[E8][E9]

Squared position error may favor an average future if visible inputs are ambiguous. For a purely illustrative input followed equally often by−10 mm or+10 mm displacement, predicting zero minimizes mean squared error. This explains a possible small-response tendency mathematically; ambiguity of this kind has not been established in the actual dataset.

Accurate point histories do not automatically supply complete physical state. Missing angular or contact state, limited independent episodes and contact-phase sampling remain possible contributors. A global network already has paths connecting objects, and small-set fitting succeeds; the evidence does not establish a mathematical inability to express collisions. Within-chunk sequential interaction is another untested design hypothesis, not a demonstrated cure.

12 How to decide the next experiment

Stop repeated seed, learning-rate and duration tuning of this shared-object version. The following are proposed checks, not completed work. Each needs its own fixed budget, data policy and decision rule.

  • Separate short-term learning from accumulation: with true current inputs, score a fixed short horizon on training and separate development scenes, stratified before, around and after contact. Large short-term training error calls for learning/fit diagnosis; accurate short-term forecasts followed by rapid autonomous deterioration support prioritizing feedback stability.
  • Test state sufficiency: under matched data splits and budgets, compare point history with additional simulator velocities/angular velocities. Added privileged state is diagnostic; if it helps, determine whether practical observations can recover it before claiming deployment value.
  • Only if preceding evidence supports it, test sequential state updates that recompute object relations from each predicted step. Preserve inputs, objective and budget where feasible; use both contact measures and the stationary reference. Sequential updating is not a guarantee of success.
  • Do not select another method on the already reported heldout set. Use a separate development scope and new heldout scenes after fixing the next method.
Further investment needs evidence that distinguishes causes. The completed work narrows the failure modes; it does not yet deliver reliable collision prediction in new scenes.

13 Verification and use limitations

Fresh collection checks include exact proposal/replay state agreement, timestamps, coordinate extraction, first-contact tables and original/shifted query seams. Independent data checks use a separate coordinate/index path; they do not independently reexecute physics to prove simulator fidelity.[E10]

Independent final scoring compares 2155910 scalars with maximum absolute difference 5.6846×10⁻¹⁷m. It hashes 2632 forecast archives,188 prepared archives and 96 contact archives, and compares 26320 input/ground-truth arrays. It independently recomputes main contact measures, raw ADE/FDE/motion/length, stationary/constant-velocity references, intervals and the continuation rule, with no training. It does not independently recompute every tipping event, contact-rank statistic, residual decomposition, training curve or performance timing.[E9]

Separate CPU reconstruction verifies all training sample traces and final sampling states. The 28620 copied training forecast archives match 36 saved result manifests. These establish provenance and consistency, not physical correctness. Public viewers retain every valid case, all three training seeds, selected/final checkpoints and invalid-draw ledgers, without smoothing or geometry correction.[E10]
