How flow enters robot decisions

Separate state, plans and action consequences before transferring a design.

1 · Question and current assessment

The negative pilots show weak use of past point motion and no stable actor benefit from past completion; they do not establish that flow is uninformative. Eight full-text methods are checked against future planning, representation supervision, and action-conditioned dynamics. Scope covers manipulation point tracks and physical scene flow, not the distributional velocity field of flow matching. Retrieval: 2026-10-19; source versions are pinned. This is a focused, non-exhaustive review.

2 · Where prior work uses flow

3 · Reconstructing the reasoning

Our reconstruction: trajectories preserve local geometry and progress without rendering all appearance. They can specify what should happen, shape a future-aware representation, or predict what happens under a candidate action. These are distinct learning problems.

4 · Core intuition

Flow may carry executable progress, missing state, or action consequences. Tokens alone do not ensure use. Test historical-state information separately from future-plan guidance.

5 · Pipelines and evidence

Method facts come from the papers; transfer suggestions are our interpretation. Expand a row.

ATM2024 · Future 2D any-point tracks

Method：Tracker pseudo-labels pretrain a future-track predictor. Predicted tracks enter both the policy Transformer and the final action MLP, alongside images.

Evidence：LIBERO-Goal: early-only 5.33%, late-only 72.83%, both 77.83% (Table III).

Boundary：Future plans and video pretraining differ from observed history. This ablation does not guarantee the same effect in ACT.

Our transfer assessment：Test an explicit plan interface near the action head.

ATM · IV-A/B; V-C, Table III; VI
Im2Flow2Act2024 · Future object 2D tracks, visibility, initial 3D anchors

Method：Human videos train task-flow generation; 4,800 simulated play episodes train execution. Online tracking and temporal alignment identify the remaining plan for a diffusion action head.

Evidence：Object motion bridges embodiments; play data pairs actions with their effects.

Boundary：This is not full 3D scene flow. The cloth simulation exposes grasp precision failures despite plausible generated motion.

Our transfer assessment：Borrow progress alignment and grasp-feasibility checks.

Im2Flow2Act · 3.1–3.2; 4.3; Appendix A/B/D/E
3D Flow Diffusion Policy2025 · Future 3D displacement of scene query points

Method：PointNet features condition 16-step flow diffusion. Temporal convolution and pooling encode the predicted plan, concatenated with observation features for action diffusion. Labels use meshes in simulation and CoTracker plus depth on hardware.

Evidence：Eight hardware tasks: 27.5%→56.9%; 50 demos and 20 evaluation trials per task. Scene sampling improves Hammer from 48.3% to 68.3%.

Boundary：Simulation uses one training seed. Evaluation seeds do not establish training-seed robustness; capacity is not fully isolated.

Our transfer assessment：A close template for future scene-flow conditioning.

3D Flow Diffusion Policy · IV-B–E; V-A/B, Tables I–III
RoboFlow4D2026 · Primarily future gripper 3D tracks

Method：RGB history and language condition FlowDiT with distilled VGGT geometry. A frozen planner supplies MLP/attention-pooled flow to DP or DiT. Gripper-state transitions define atomic tasks.

Evidence：LIBERO DP average: 78.9%→85.1%. Labels are gripper-centric, with scene-flow fallback when the gripper is absent.

Boundary：Gripper plans do not establish object interaction dynamics. Progress-resampled tracks do not preserve physical velocity.

Our transfer assessment：Borrow the stable plan interface; narrow the dynamics claim.

RoboFlow4D · 3.2–3.5; 4.2–4.4; Appendix C/D
3PoinTr2026 · Dense future 3D tracks excluding embodiment points

Method：Stereo-derived 3D tracks supervise a small Transformer with visible-position L1. A frozen predictor feeds per-track embeddings through Perceiver IO to a policy conditioned only on those tracks.

Evidence：With 20 robot demos, Fold Sock scores 9/10 versus DP3's 2/10; hardware evaluation uses only ten trials per task.

Boundary：Fixed-camera, single-task experiments execute open-loop and cannot recover from unexpected dynamics.

Our transfer assessment：A learned query can compress many trajectories.

3PoinTr · III; IV-B/E, Table III; VI
EgoWAM2026 · Camera-stabilized future dense 3D flow

Method：Action and future tokens share the trunk. A future-flow head shapes that trunk and is discarded at inference. Track4World labels are stabilized with Aria VIO and filtered for low-motion noise.

Evidence：Each task uses 300–360 robot demos and roughly 10:1 human data. Human action/flow supervision ablations favor their combination.

Boundary：Its transfer setting differs from 16 same-garment episodes and past completion; DINO is stronger on some OOD splits.

Our transfer assessment：Borrow future targets and supervision of the shared trunk.

EgoWAM · 3.2; 4.1–4.3; 5; Appendix A.2/B.2
PointWorld2026 · Candidate robot flow in; scene response flow out

Method：Current scene points and FK-generated candidate gripper trajectories predict ten-step scene displacement. Training combines motion weights, robust loss, uncertainty and visibility; MPPI scores predicted point goals.

Evidence：Flow represents both action geometry and scored outcomes. Motion weighting alone amplifies noisy labels.

Boundary：The appendix assumes a static initial scene and realized robot paths, limiting moving initial conditions.

Our transfer assessment：Test action-dependent responses, beyond aggregate EPE.

PointWorld · 3.1–3.2; 5.1–5.2; Appendix A.1/A.4
FlowDreamer2025 / RA-L 2026 · Action-conditioned next-step dense 3D flow

Method：An action-conditioned U-Net predicts flow, concatenated with depth and RGB latents for next-frame diffusion. Joint flow/generation losses support image-scored MPC.

Evidence：Reversing flow while holding actions fixed degrades generated frames, supporting downstream use.

Boundary：Missing velocity and noisy real pseudo-labels limit results. The generator conditions on flow; it is not an explicit geometric warp renderer.

Our transfer assessment：Downstream use and adequate state information both matter.

6 · Four interfaces in compact notation

History: a = π(o, h_flow)
Plan: F̂ = G(o, h, g); a = π(o, F̂)
Auxiliary: z = E(o, h); L = L_action + λL_future-flow
Dynamics: F̂ = D(o, h, a_candidate); a* = argmin J(F̂, a)

Only the last interface explicitly compares candidate-action effects. Progress-normalized trajectories are not physical-time velocities.

7 · Evidence and the local pilots

Observed local evidence: four-seed short pilots use 16/4 same-garment episodes. At 2000 updates ACT/Tracks MSE is 0.065236/0.069617 rad²; Tracks−Geometry is +0.000087. At 1500 updates zeroing past motion changes MSE by only +0.0446%. These are simulator GT tracks. Strong completion remains worse than 3NN on movers (30.525 vs 27.578 mm); connected auxiliary gradients do not improve the final actor comparison. The auxiliary path does not directly supervise the main ACT Transformer. See the existing analysis.

Implementation cautions: 3D FDP's denoising prose and target notation need code verification. RoboFlow4D's γ>1 time-warp formula densifies the start, despite prose claiming the end.

8 · Takeaways: five / three / one

Five: Flow can encode state, plans, or consequences. It needs an explicit consumption path. Future prediction differs from past completion. Data, motion selection and gradients matter. Control claims need interpretable rollout evaluation.

Three: Separate state from plans. Test use. Match the evaluation to the claim.

One: Identify the decision problem flow solves.

9 · The weakest assumption

The fragile transfer assumption is that flow adds decision-relevant information given existing RGB and proprioception. Good completion may be interpolation; weak sensitivity may reflect redundancy or failed use. Current results do not identify one unique cause.

10 · Minimal discriminating experiments (proposed)

First isolate target direction and gradient path: matched no-aux, past-completion, future-prediction, and future-prediction with detached trunk gradients. Keep actor, decoder layout, inputs, masks, batches and initialization matched; calibrate gradients on training data only. Use at least three training seeds and a fixed budget. Hold out episodes/families; report full action chunks, offsets, motion/contact strata and suitable flow baselines. Future-connected must beat both past and detached controls to support its proposed mechanism.

Then test a separately validated frozen planner with late action conditioning. Use predicted plans consistently in training and deployment. GT future plans are a privileged diagnostic ceiling, never a deployable result. Rollouts require accepted resets and expert controls. No training has been started.

11 · Strongest counterexample

Pair scenes with matched visible geometry, task and candidate actions but opposite historical velocities. Measure contact timing and post-contact response. Matched training/evaluation controls must distinguish true history from wrong history without relying solely on out-of-distribution deletion. Unobserved material parameters may remain unidentifiable.

12 · A possible research question

Proposal, unimplemented and not established as novel: use historical flow to predict how feasible candidate actions change post-contact outcomes. Test response differences, action ranking, then control. Relevant tools include system identification and decision-focused model learning. PointWorld already predicts action-conditioned flow and this project already has paired-response diagnostics; adding history or predicting responses alone is not a novelty claim.
